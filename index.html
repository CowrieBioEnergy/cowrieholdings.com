The Three Ps — proximity, perspective, pursuit
A Legacy Symbol

A future-forward
mandate.

The cowrie shell — once traded as currency across Africa, Asia, and Oceania — embodies the power of patient, purposeful capital. Though small, it moved world economies and became a visual mainstay across cultures.

It is also a symbol of wealth, trade, and resilience. A small object that proved durable enough to outlast the empires that used it.

We don't just provide capital — we provide the operational excellence required to sustain growth. Where others see risk, we see architecture waiting to be built.

Across both mandates, our role is the same: relational mentor, operational partner, dot connector. Whether we're acquiring a 30-year-old service business in the Midwest or backing a founder building Africa's next financial rail, we believe the work of compounding capital is inseparable from the work of compounding trust.

That belief shapes how we underwrite, how we operate, and how long we stay. We are in the business of preserving legacies through modern stewardship — and of building new ones, slowly, on solid ground.

Investment Thesis

We back operators
with the Three Ps.

Across both mandates, we look for the same human qualities. Whether we're evaluating a founder in Lagos or a seller in Cleveland, our north star for every investment decision is the same — three traits that, in our experience, separate the operators who build durable enterprises from those who don't.

P.
Proximity

Lived experience — geographic or social familiarity with the fundamental problem. The ability to feel impact in real-time, as it unfolds on the ground.

P.
Perspective

An ability to look at the problem from multiple angles, identify viable solutions, and understand what needs to happen next to crack it open.

P.
Pursuit

A dedication to seeing possibility amid scarcity — and the willingness to test, iterate, and scale tactical solutions that transcend borders.
